The 5 violation families

Each family maps to published ad-platform or regulator guidance. Every finding names the policy, quotes the full offending sentence, and ships with fix guidance.

1. Unsubstantiated superlatives

Google/Meta — Misleading Claims & Exaggerated Guarantees

Catches best, #1, top-rated, finest, cheapest, lowest prices, nobody beats our prices, unbeatable — with smart exclusions so "we do our best" and "best practices" don't get flagged.

2. Absolute guarantees

Google/Meta — Misleading Claims & Exaggerated Guarantees

Catches money-back, 100% guaranteed, iron-clad guarantee, that's a promise, on the house, satisfaction guaranteed — promises that need visible terms or they read as exaggerated guarantees.

3. Financial & savings claims

Google/Meta — Misleading Claims & Exaggerated Guarantees

Catches save you up to $X, save $500, money back in your pocket, pays for itself, cut your bills by X% — projected-savings claims that need a disclosed basis.

4. Personal attributes

Meta/Facebook — Personal Attributes (Section 4.6.2)

Catches are you struggling…, tired of YOUR old AC, you're overpaying, if you're like most homeowners, homeowners like you, your family's safety — Meta bans asserting or implying the viewer's personal attributes.

5. Safety claims

EPA, FTC Regulations & Ad Networks — Safety Claims Standards

Catches 100% safe, completely safe, harmless to children and pets, chemical-free, all-natural, non-toxic, zero risk, kills … on contact, wipes out … on contact — absolute safety/efficacy claims regulators prohibit.

How scoring works

No mystery math. The score is arithmetic on the findings — and the verdict is one of three labels. The tool pre-screens; it never blesses an ad as safe to launch.

HIGH RISK
any HIGH finding
NEEDS REVIEW
MEDIUM finding or gray-zone triggers
CLEAR
no findings, no triggers
Exactly how is the score computed?
Start at 100. Subtract 20 per HIGH finding, 8 per MEDIUM finding. Two or more HIGH findings caps the score at 60. Floor is 40. Verdict: any HIGH → HIGH RISK; otherwise any MEDIUM or gray-zone trigger → NEEDS REVIEW; otherwise CLEAR.
What is the gray zone?
Promotional triggers like "free", "guarantee", "limited time", or a bare percentage that don't match a hard violation pattern. They land in a "needs human review" bucket with the triggers listed — a person decides, not the engine.
How does the license check work?
A case-sensitive regex looks for formatted credentials — letters, #, digits — like ROC #276078 or TECL #19787. Plain numbers ("10 years in business") never match. If nothing matches, the report says so plainly instead of inventing a license.

Landing Page Audit

Paste the page's visible text — or the raw HTML, which is stripped automatically (<title>, headlines, and tel: links are read directly). Six deterministic check groups, same HIGH RISK / NEEDS REVIEW / CLEAR verdicts, same full-sentence evidence. Run a page audit →

📞

Phone extraction & consistency

Finds every US-format number plus all tel: links. The link wins on conflict — a stale visible number next to a current tap-to-call link gets flagged HIGH.

🪪

License credential detection

State patterns (OR CCB, CA CSLB, AZ ROC, WA, TX, FL…) plus generic numbered-license formats. A bare "licensed" with no credential is never quoted as proof — the report says "couldn't confirm — verify manually."

🏷️

Business name consistency

Compares the page title/headline name against body mentions (case- and punctuation-insensitive). Mismatches confuse visitors and hurt local search.

👆

Call-to-action inventory

Counts call, quote, booking, contact, and form CTAs. Zero CTAs is a HIGH finding — visitors are never told what to do next.

⭐

Trust signals

Years in business, reviews and ratings, guarantees and warranties, insured/bonded wording — recorded as strengths when found, never invented.

📍

Service area signal

Looks for "serving" areas, street addresses, and ZIP codes. A page that never says where it works gets flagged.

Deterministic, not AI

An AI audit can say something different every run and hallucinate evidence. A rule engine can't. Same ad in, same verdict out — and when it misses, the fix is legible: add a pattern, add a regression test, bump the version.
